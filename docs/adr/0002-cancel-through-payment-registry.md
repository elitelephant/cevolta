# Cancellation goes through the Payment Registry, not only the Smart Wallet

The original design had the Payer cancel purely inside their own Smart Wallet (revoking the Policy Signer), leaving the Payment Registry's status stale until the next renewal attempt failed. Soroban allows only one `InvokeHostFunctionOp` per transaction, so we made `cancel()` on the Payment Registry the single cancellation entry point: the Payer signs it with their normal wallet signer (not the Policy Signer), and within that one invocation the Registry updates status to `cancelled` and cross-contract-calls the Smart Wallet to deactivate the Policy Signer. This keeps cancellation entirely Payer-initiated and Payer-signed, unchanged from the original security property that it never depends on the Payee or the protocol. It also makes the Registry's status immediately consistent instead of eventually consistent. The trade-off: the Smart Wallet must expose a callable "deactivate Policy Signer" entry point the Registry is allowed to invoke, which is a small additional surface on the wallet side.
