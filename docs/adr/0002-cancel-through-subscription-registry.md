# Cancellation goes through the Subscription Registry, not only the Smart Wallet

The original design had the Subscriber cancel purely inside their own Smart Wallet (revoking the Policy Signer), leaving the Subscription Registry's status stale until the next renewal attempt failed. Soroban allows only one `InvokeHostFunctionOp` per transaction, so we made `cancel()` on the Subscription Registry the single cancellation entry point: the Subscriber signs it with their normal wallet signer (not the Policy Signer), and within that one invocation the Registry updates status to `cancelled` and cross-contract-calls the Smart Wallet to deactivate the Policy Signer. This keeps cancellation entirely Subscriber-initiated and Subscriber-signed — unchanged from the original security property that it never depends on the Merchant or the protocol — while making the Registry's status immediately consistent instead of eventually consistent. The trade-off: the Smart Wallet must expose a callable "deactivate Policy Signer" entry point the Registry is allowed to invoke, which is a small additional surface on the wallet side.
